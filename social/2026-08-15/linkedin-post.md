The most expensive dollar in early retirement is the one that crosses $84,600.

The enhanced ACA subsidies expired December 31, 2025. For 2026, the old cliff is back: subsidies end abruptly at 400% of the federal poverty level — about $62,600 of income for a single person, $84,600 for a couple.

Under the line, a couple's benchmark silver premium is capped at 9.96% of income — roughly $8,400 a year, maximum. One dollar over, the cap vanishes and they pay full sticker price. For a couple in their early sixties, that's routinely a multiple of the capped amount. No phase-out. A cliff.

Here's the collision nobody warns you about: standard gap-years advice says fill your low tax brackets with Roth conversions — up to $133,000 of gross income for a couple before anything is taxed above 12%. But if you're pre-65 on a marketplace plan, the binding number isn't $133,000. It's $84,600. Convert to the top of the bracket and the marginal cost isn't 12% — it's 12% plus the entire lost subsidy.

The fix is choosing which years do which job: subsidy years under the line, conversion years after 65 — or one deliberate over-the-cliff year surrounded by tight ones. Drifting slightly over every year is the only truly wrong answer.

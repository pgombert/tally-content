There's a federal tax rate of exactly 0%, and most people who qualify for it never use it.

In 2026, long-term capital gains are taxed at 0% as long as taxable income stays under $49,450 (single) or $98,900 (married filing jointly). Those are IRS numbers, adjusted for inflation every year.

The part that surprises people: the threshold is taxable income, after deductions. A retired couple, both 65+, gets a $32,200 standard deduction plus the new $6,000-per-person senior deduction. If their only income is long-term gains, they could realize $143,100 in 2026 and owe zero federal income tax on it.

The move is called tax-gain harvesting. Sell the appreciated fund, realize the gain at 0%, buy it back the same day — the wash-sale rule only applies to losses. Your cost basis resets higher, and that gain never gets taxed. Ever.

The catch is that it's a use-it-or-lose-it allowance that resets in January, and the traps are real: state taxes, ACA subsidy clawbacks, Medicare IRMAA, and the fact that a Roth conversion competes for the same bracket space.

So it's a December decision, not an April one. You have to know your income before the year closes.

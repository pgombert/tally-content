A reader told me he's kept a meticulous budget in YNAB for six years — and still has no idea when he can retire.

That's not a discipline problem. It's an architecture problem.

In most budgeting apps, your 401(k) and brokerage accounts are "tracking accounts": one number, the balance. No holdings, no returns, no model of what happens when you stop contributing and start withdrawing. The biggest financial project of your life shows up as a line that goes up.

So people run two tools — a budgeting app for the month, a retirement planner for the decades. I did it myself for years. The flaw: the planner needs your annual spending as an input, and you type in a guess at the very number your budgeting app tracks to the penny. Spending drifts. The plan goes stale. The two never check each other.

Your real spending is the single best input a retirement forecast can have. That conviction is why I built Tally as one app: the transactions that run this month's budget also run a lifetime, tax-aware forecast — state taxes, Social Security timing, Roth conversions, Monte Carlo — down to a runway date that moves when your life does.

Your budgeting app has never met your retirement plan.

When Mint shut down in 2024, the personal finance market split in two. One camp tracks today's money: YNAB, Monarch. The other models future money: Boldin, ProjectionLab. Each is good at its half. Neither can do the other's job — ProjectionLab says so itself: it's a planner, not a budgeting app.

So most people who want the full picture run both. At current list prices, that stack costs $229 to $253 a year.

The subscriptions aren't the real problem. The gap between the apps is.

A retirement forecast has one input that matters more than any other: what you actually spend. In a two-app world, that number gets typed into the planner by hand, from memory. Meanwhile the budget app records the truth, and nobody reconciles them. A spending guess that's off by $800 a month makes a lifetime projection wrong by hundreds of thousands of dollars. The error compounds silently for decades.

I built Tally because I think today's groceries and 2055's runway are the same dollars on one timeline, and the software should treat them that way: synced accounts feeding a tax-aware lifetime forecast, automatically.

This week I published the full comparison — pricing, a checklist for evaluating any one-app replacement, and the honest section on when two apps still win.

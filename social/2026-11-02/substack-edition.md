# Your 2027 budget is already written. It's in your bank statements.

Every January I used to do the same thing: open a fresh spreadsheet, type optimistic numbers into category boxes, and call it a budget. By March it was fiction. The problem wasn't discipline. The problem was that I was guessing, and my bank account already knew the real numbers.

I kept picking grocery numbers that described the person I wanted to be, not the person who actually buys the groceries. The spreadsheet never argued with me. Reality did, every month, until I stopped opening the spreadsheet at all.

That's why I think November — not January — is budget season. By now you have ten full months of 2026 spending on record. Add November and December from last year and you have a complete picture of what a year of your life actually costs. That picture is the most honest budget draft you will ever get. You don't have to invent next year's budget. You have to read it, adjust it for what's changing, and commit to it.

This week's post on the blog walks through the whole process. The short version:

**Pull 12 months of actuals first.** Every account you spend from, trailing twelve months. Three totals: what came in, what went out, where it went. No planning until the record is straight.

**Sort into fixed, variable, irregular.** Fixed carries over nearly as-is. Variable gets budgeted at your 12-month average — not your proudest month. Irregular is the one that changes everything: car registration, gifts, annual renewals, repairs. Total a year of those and most households find hundreds of dollars a month hiding in "one-offs."

**Build the year, then the months.** This is the structural change that makes everything else work. A monthly budget treats all twelve months as identical, and they aren't — December costs more than February, June has the insurance premium, summer has the trips. An annual budget starts with what the whole year should cost, then breaks it into months that are allowed to differ. The $900 premium in June isn't a blown budget. It was planned in November. No month in 2027 should surprise you.

**Update what changes in January.** A few numbers are already set. Social Security benefits rise 3.6% in 2027 — that COLA was announced in October. The 2027 HSA limits are out too: $4,500 self-only, $9,000 family. The 2027 401(k) and IRA limits come from the IRS around mid-November; for 2026 they're $24,500 and $7,500, so if you budget to max out, expect those lines to move.

**Make irregular expenses a monthly number.** $6,000 a year of "surprises" is just $500 a month with bad scheduling. Fund it monthly, into a holding category or a separate account, and December stops being an emergency. Of everything on this list, this habit removes the most stress per minute of effort.

**Make saving a line item, not a leftover.** Decide the number on purpose — retirement, taxable investing, cash buffer — instead of letting it be whatever survives the month. That one change is most of what separates budgets that build wealth from budgets that just track it.

**Schedule the review.** A budget isn't a January ritual; it's a standing agreement you check against reality. Monthly works for most people. Two rules: adjust when life changes, not when one month goes sideways, and never treat a blown category as a moral failing. It's an instrument panel, not a report card.

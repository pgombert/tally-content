The most common question in my inbox this year: "What should I look for in a Monarch Money alternative?"

My first answer is always the same — maybe nothing. Monarch is a well-built app. If it's working for you, switching costs an afternoon and buys you regret.

But the question behind the question is usually this: people set up a budget, categorize a year of spending, and then hit the question every budget eventually leads to. Am I going to be okay? And they discover that answer lives in a $199.99/year tier, or in a second app entirely.

So this week I published the comparison guide I'd want to read, with the obvious disclosure up front: I build a competing product, so every claim is specific and checkable.

The one thing I'd tell anyone evaluating any forecasting tool, including mine: read how it handles taxes. Flat-rate assumptions — "assume 20% on withdrawals" — are fine for a napkin. But real retirements fill real brackets. RMDs arrive whether you planned them or not. States take 0% to double digits. Roth conversion math exists entirely in the gap between your bracket now and your bracket at 73. A flat rate can't see any of it.

A forecast that skips the tax math isn't wrong. It's confident in a way it hasn't earned.

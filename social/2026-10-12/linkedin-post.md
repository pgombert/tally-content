# LinkedIn post — 2026-10-12

Here's a tax rule that surprises almost every new retiree I talk to.

Estimated tax payments are credited the day you make them. Miss the June deadline, catch up in September, and you still owe a penalty for the gap — currently 7% a year, compounded daily.

But withholding plays by a different rule. The IRS treats withheld tax as paid evenly across the entire year, no matter when it actually came out.

That asymmetry is a gift for retirees. Tax withheld from an IRA distribution in December counts as if a quarter of it arrived back in April.

So instead of mailing four vouchers on the IRS's oddly spaced calendar (April, June, September, January — two months, then three, then four), many retirees can do this: wait until December, when the year's income is no longer a guess, and have their IRA custodian withhold enough from one distribution to hit the safe harbor — 100% of last year's tax, or 110% if prior-year AGI topped $150,000.

One transaction. Zero deadlines. Penalty-proof.

I spent my career in software and now build Tally, a personal finance app that models taxes properly — federal and all 50 states — because retirement tax planning is mostly about seeing the number before it's due.

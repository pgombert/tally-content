# The Tax Deadline Nobody Tells Retirees About

A reader emailed me a version of a question I've now heard a dozen times: "I retired in March. Do I seriously have to mail the IRS a check four times a year now?"

Short answer: maybe — but probably not, if you know one obscure rule about withholding. That rule is this week's topic, because it turns a year of deadlines into a single December errand.

First, the problem. Your whole working life, taxes were somebody else's job. Payroll withheld them before you ever saw the money. The day you retire, that machinery shuts off. IRA withdrawals, brokerage gains, Roth conversions, Social Security — none of it is withheld unless you set it up. The IRS, meanwhile, still wants paying as you go. Fall behind and the underpayment penalty currently accrues at 7% a year, compounded daily.

The test is whether you'll owe $1,000 or more at filing after withholding and credits. Most retirees without withholding will.

## The safe harbors

You don't need to predict your tax bill perfectly. You need to land in one of three safe harbors, and any one of them makes you penalty-proof:

Owe less than $1,000 at filing. Or pay in at least 90% of this year's tax during the year. Or pay in 100% of *last year's* tax — 110% if your prior-year AGI was over $150,000.

That last one is the workhorse. Last year's tax is a known number on your last return. Pay it in on schedule and you're protected even if your income doubles — which makes it ideal for a big Roth conversion year. The one time it backfires is a year your income drops, like your first full year of retirement: then 90% of the (lower) current year is the cheaper target.

The deadlines, by the way, are not actually quarterly: April 15, June 15, September 15, January 15. Two months, then three, then four. The June one ambushes people annually.

## The rule that changes everything

Now the part I wish someone had told every retiree on day one.

Estimated payments are credited on the day you send them. Late is late. But withholding is treated as if it were paid evenly across the entire year — regardless of when it actually happened.

Read that again, because it's a loophole-shaped gift: tax withheld from an IRA distribution in *December* counts as if it had been trickling in since January.

So if you're taking IRA withdrawals or RMDs anyway, you can skip the vouchers entirely. In December, once you know what the year actually looked like, have your custodian withhold enough from a distribution to hit your safe harbor number. One transaction. No deadlines. Fully penalty-proof.

Around that core move, the supporting cast: Form W-4V puts withholding on your Social Security at 7%, 10%, 12%, or 22%. Form W-4P does the same for pensions. And any IRA or 401(k) distribution can carry a withholding election — most custodians will go well past the default rate if you ask.

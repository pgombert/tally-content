# LinkedIn post — 2026-11-16

The biggest tax-free gain most Americans will ever take was sized in 1997. It hasn't moved since.

Sell your main home and you can exclude up to $250,000 of profit from tax — $500,000 if you're married filing jointly. Own and live there 2 of the last 5 years, and for most sellers that's the whole tax story.

But those caps were set by the Taxpayer Relief Act of 1997 and never indexed for inflation. Home prices have multiplied several times over since. So every year, more couples who bought in the 1990s discover their gain is past $500,000 — and the part above the cap is taxable.

Three things I'd tell anyone within a few years of selling a longtime home:

Find the improvement receipts. Every documented dollar of renovations raises your basis and shrinks the taxable gain. The boring folder is worth real money.

Know your bracket. The taxable part stacks on your other income. In 2026 a married couple pays 0% on long-term gains until taxable income passes $98,900 — plenty of sellers owe far less than they fear.

Mind the calendar. A surviving spouse keeps the $500,000 cap only by selling within two years. And a big gain this year can raise Medicare premiums two years from now.

A home sale is the rare tax event you can see coming years out. Model it before you list.

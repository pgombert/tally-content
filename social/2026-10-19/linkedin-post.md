The two most credible voices in retirement research just gave answers almost a full point apart.

Bill Bengen — the man who invented the 4% rule — published a book in 2025 revising his own number to 4.7%, based on a broader portfolio than his original 1994 study.

Morningstar's latest State of Retirement Income research, built on forward-looking return projections instead of history, puts the safe starting rate for 2026 retirees at 3.9%.

Same question. Same 30-year horizon. Nearly a point of disagreement.

And if you plan to retire early, neither number is yours. The 4% rule was validated against 30-year retirements. Retire at 45 and your money has to last 50. Backtests of long horizons show the success rate of 4% dropping meaningfully — at some allocations, sharply.

I spent years modeling this in spreadsheets before building Tally, and the lesson I keep relearning: a withdrawal rate compresses your whole financial life into one number, and the compression throws away what decides the outcome. Taxes by account type. Roth conversion windows. Social Security timing. Health insurance before 65. The sequence of returns in your first decade.

25x your spending is a fine milestone. It is not a plan. A plan is your accounts, your taxes, your dates — stress-tested against thousands of market sequences, not one average line.

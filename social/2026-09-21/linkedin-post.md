# LinkedIn post — 2026-09-21

A rule passed in 2022 finally showed up in paychecks this year, and I keep meeting people over 50 who found out about it from a two-line payroll email.

Since January 1: if your FICA wages were over $150,000 in 2025, every catch-up contribution to your 401(k), 403(b), or governmental 457(b) must be Roth. After-tax. No pre-tax option. The IRS finalized the regulations last September.

The cost is real. An $8,000 catch-up used to cut your federal bill by $1,920 in the 24% bracket. That deduction is gone, and it comes out of this year's withholding.

But the disaster framing misses three things.

Roth dollars grow tax-free permanently. Roth 401(k) balances have had no RMDs since 2024, while pre-tax money faces forced withdrawals at 73 or 75. And if you turn 60 to 63 this year, your catch-up is $11,250, not $8,000 — a four-year window most people don't know exists.

The trap to check today: if your plan doesn't offer a Roth option, affected high earners can't make catch-up contributions at all until it adds one. One question to HR settles it.

Whether forced Roth hurts you or quietly helps you depends on one number: the gap between your bracket now and your bracket in retirement. That's a forecast, not a rule of thumb.

# LinkedIn post — 2026-09-14

The most expensive belief in retirement planning: "my money is locked up until 59½."

It isn't. The 10% early-withdrawal penalty has four legal exits, and I keep meeting people who worked years longer than they wanted to because nobody showed them the doors.

The rule of 55: leave your job in or after the year you turn 55, and that employer's 401(k) opens penalty-free. The classic mistake is rolling it into an IRA "to simplify" — that locks the door behind you.

72(t) payments: commit to a fixed annual draw from your IRA, calculated by IRS formula, and the penalty disappears at any age. A 50-year-old with $500K can draw about $30K a year. The catch: you're locked in until 59½, and breaking the schedule triggers penalties retroactively on every dollar taken.

The Roth conversion ladder: convert a year of spending annually, wait five tax years per conversion, and you've built a conveyor belt of penalty-free money. Needs a five-year bridge and careful tax sizing.

The governmental 457(b): if you have one, there's no early-withdrawal penalty after you separate. At any age. Most people holding these plans don't know.

The penalty is the easy part. The hard part is that every door turns pre-tax money into taxable income — so they compete with your brackets, your ACA subsidy, and each other. That sequencing is where early retirements are won or lost.

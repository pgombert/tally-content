The most expensive advice in retirement planning is free, everywhere, and sounds prudent:

"Spend your taxable account first. Save the IRA. Touch the Roth last."

Here's what that order actually does for a typical 65-year-old couple.

Years 1–8: they live off the brokerage account. Taxable income near zero. They're proud of the $0 tax bills. But in 2026 a 65+ couple gets $44,200 in deductions ($32,200 standard + $6,000 senior deduction each). Deductions don't carry forward. Eight years of 0% brackets expire unused.

Year 9: RMDs arrive at 73. The "preserved" $900K IRA has grown to roughly $1.4M. First forced withdrawal: about $54K, stacked on Social Security (up to 85% taxable). Welcome to the 22% bracket, permanently.

They didn't avoid the tax. They deferred it into the one decade they can't control it.

The better question isn't "which account first?" It's "how much income can I recognize this year at rates I'll never see again?" Usually the answer is a blend: IRA withdrawals or Roth conversions to the top of the cheap brackets, brokerage sales inside the 0% capital-gains zone ($98,900 taxable income, married filing jointly, 2026).

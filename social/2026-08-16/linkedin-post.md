# LinkedIn post — 2026-08-16

The biggest retirement decision most people make on gut feel: when to claim Social Security.

The 2026 numbers, for anyone born in 1960 or later:

Claim at 62, and you get 70% of your benefit. Permanently.
Claim at 67 (full retirement age), and you get 100%.
Claim at 70, and delayed credits take you to 124%.

That makes the age-70 check about 77% larger than the age-62 check. Same earnings record. Same person. A $2,000 benefit becomes either $1,400 or $2,480 a month.

The breakeven lands around age 80 — but the breakeven chart is the wrong tool. It ignores the three things that actually decide this:

Survivors. When one spouse dies, the smaller check stops. The higher earner's claiming age sets the widow's or widower's income for decades.

Taxes. Up to 85% of benefits can be taxable, and the thresholds haven't moved since the 1980s. Claiming age, IRA withdrawals, and Roth conversions all interact.

Longevity risk. The bad outcome isn't dying at 75 with money left. It's being 95 without it. A bigger check is inflation-adjusted income that can't run out.

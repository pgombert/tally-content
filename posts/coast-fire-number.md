# What Is Coast FIRE? How to Calculate Your Coast FIRE Number

There's a point on the way to retirement that almost nobody notices when they cross it. It's the day your savings become big enough that, even if you never added another dollar, compounding alone would carry you to a fully funded retirement.

The FIRE community calls that point Coast FIRE. You haven't retired. You still work, still earn, still pay the mortgage. But the hardest job in personal finance — funding your old age — is already done. Everything you earn from that day forward only has to cover the life you're living now.

Most people who've hit Coast FIRE don't know it. The math isn't hard, but you have to actually run it. Let's run it.

## The idea in one sentence

Coast FIRE means your current retirement savings, left alone to grow, will reach your retirement number by your retirement date — with no further contributions.

Note what it doesn't mean. It doesn't mean you stop working. It doesn't mean you touch the money. It means you could stop *saving for retirement* and still retire on schedule. Some people use that freedom to take a lower-stress job. Some drop to four days a week. Some just keep saving anyway and move their retirement date up. The point is that saving becomes a choice instead of a requirement.

## Step 1: Find your retirement number

Start at the end. How much do you need on the day you retire?

The common shortcut is the 25x rule, which is just the 4% rule run backward: take your expected annual spending in retirement and multiply by 25. If you expect to spend $60,000 a year, that's $1.5 million. If you expect $40,000, it's $1 million.

Two honest caveats. First, your spending in retirement is not your spending now — taxes change, the mortgage may be gone, healthcare usually costs more, especially if you retire before Medicare eligibility at 65. Second, 25x is a rule of thumb built on a 30-year retirement. It's a fine starting point and a poor finishing point. We'll fix that in step 4.

## Step 2: Discount it back to today

Here's the formula:

**Coast FIRE number = retirement number ÷ (1 + growth rate) ^ years until retirement**

The growth rate matters more than anything else in this calculation, and this is where people fool themselves. If you use a nominal return — say, the headline long-run stock market average — you're ignoring inflation, and your "retirement number" will buy a lot less by the time you get there. The cleaner approach is to work in today's dollars and use a *real* (after-inflation) return. A real return of around 5% is a common planning assumption for a stock-heavy portfolio. Conservative planners use 3–4%. Pick one you can defend, and know that your answer is only as good as this guess.

## Step 3: Run the numbers

Say your retirement number is $1.5 million in today's dollars, you'll retire at 65, and you assume 5% real growth. Here's what you'd need invested today:

| Your age now | Years to grow | Coast FIRE number |
|---|---|---|
| 35 | 30 | ~$347,000 |
| 40 | 25 | ~$443,000 |
| 45 | 20 | ~$565,000 |
| 50 | 15 | ~$722,000 |
| 55 | 10 | ~$921,000 |

Read that table twice, because it says two things. The first is encouraging: a 45-year-old with $565,000 invested has, under these assumptions, already funded a $60,000-a-year retirement. No more contributions required. $565,000 compounding at 5% real for 20 years is about $1.5 million in today's dollars.

The second is the quiet brutality of time. The 35-year-old needs $347,000; the 55-year-old needs $921,000 for the same outcome. Every year you wait, the number climbs, because you're buying fewer years of compounding.

Another way to see it: $300,000 invested at 40 grows to roughly $1 million by 65 at 5% real, untouched. $500,000 at 45 grows to roughly $1.3 million. The portfolio does the heavy lifting — but only if you give it the years.

## Step 4: Stress-test it — this is the step that matters

Everything above is a single straight line: one growth rate, one spending number, one retirement date. Real life is none of those things. Before you act on a Coast FIRE calculation — before you take the easier job or cut your savings rate — you should pressure-test it from at least four directions.

**Sequence and volatility.** A 5% average return is not 5% every year. Two bad decades have happened before and will happen again. A Monte Carlo simulation, which runs your plan through thousands of randomized market sequences, tells you something a straight-line projection can't: not "will this work on average" but "how often does this fail."

**Taxes.** $1.5 million in a traditional 401(k) is not $1.5 million. Withdrawals from pre-tax accounts are taxed as ordinary income, and once you reach RMD age, withdrawals stop being optional. The same balance split across pre-tax, Roth, and taxable accounts can support meaningfully different spending levels. A Coast FIRE number that ignores taxes is overstated — sometimes by six figures.

**Social Security.** Most Coast FIRE math quietly ignores it, which makes the target harder than it needs to be. For anyone born in 1960 or later, full retirement age is 67, and claiming earlier or later changes the benefit substantially. Even a partial benefit lowers the portfolio withdrawal rate your savings must sustain.

**Your actual spending.** Not a guess — your real categories, projected forward, with healthcare modeled honestly for any gap years before 65.

## What coasting actually changes

Suppose you run the numbers, stress-test them, and you're there. What now?

For 2026, the IRS allows up to $24,500 in employee 401(k) contributions and $7,500 in IRA contributions. At Coast FIRE, you're no longer obligated to chase those limits. That freed-up cash flow can fund a career change, a sabbatical fund, college accounts, or simply a less tightly wound life. Many people keep contributing at least to their employer match — leaving matching dollars on the table is hard to justify — but the pressure is gone.

And that's the real payoff. Coast FIRE isn't a finish line; it's a load-bearing fact about your finances that changes what your paycheck is for. People describe the shift the same way: work gets lighter when it's funding your life instead of your entire future.

Your Coast FIRE number is your retirement number discounted back to today at a real growth rate you believe. The one-line formula gets you an estimate in five minutes.

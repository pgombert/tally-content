Most budgets are built backwards. You sit down in January, guess what a reasonable grocery number looks like, and spend the next eleven months feeling vaguely guilty about it.

There is a better way, and November is the month to do it. By now you have ten full months of real 2026 spending on the books. That data is the most honest budget draft you will ever get. You don't need to invent your 2027 budget. You need to read it, adjust it, and commit to it.

Here is the whole process, step by step.

## Why an annual budget beats twelve monthly ones

A monthly budget treats every month as identical. Your life doesn't work that way. Insurance premiums land twice a year. Property taxes come due in installments. December costs more than February. Summer has camps and travel; March has almost nothing.

An annual budget starts with the year as the unit — here is what 2027 should cost, in total — and then breaks that into months that are allowed to be different from each other. A $900 insurance bill in June isn't a failure of June's budget. It was planned in November.

This is also the only budget structure that connects cleanly to long-term planning. If you know what a year of your life costs, you know what ten years cost, and you can start asking real questions about how long your money lasts.

## Step 1: Pull your last 12 months of actual spending

Before you plan a single dollar, get the record straight. Export or sync every account you spend from — checking, credit cards, the forgotten store card — and look at the trailing 12 months, November 2025 through October 2026.

You are looking for three totals: what you earned, what you spent, and where it went by category.

If your transactions live in one place and are categorized, this takes minutes. If they don't, this is the slow step, and it is worth doing anyway. (It is the step Tally was built for: connect accounts once, let the AI categorize, review what it got wrong.) However you do it, don't skip it. A budget built on guesses inherits all the guesswork.

## Step 2: Sort every category into fixed, variable, or irregular

Now sort your categories into three buckets.

Fixed costs are the same every month: rent or mortgage, insurance paid monthly, internet, loan payments. These transfer into 2027 almost unchanged, except where you know a number is rising — more on that below.

Variable costs happen every month but move around: groceries, fuel, dining out, utilities. For these, use your 12-month average, not your best month. If groceries averaged $740, budgeting $550 because it feels disciplined is how budgets die in February.

Irregular costs are the ambush category: car registration, holiday gifts, annual subscriptions, home repairs, vet bills. Individually they look like one-offs. Across a year they are remarkably consistent. Total them for the trailing 12 months. Most households find this number is far larger than they assumed — often several hundred dollars a month in disguise.

## Step 3: Build the year first, then the months

With your three buckets totaled, draft the 2027 year: income at the top, then fixed, variable, and irregular spending, then what's left for saving and investing.

Then break the year into months that reflect reality. Put the insurance premium in the month it actually bills. Put gifts in November and December. Put the property tax installments where they land. The goal is that no month in 2027 surprises you, because every lumpy expense already has a home.

## Step 4: Update the numbers that change in January

A 2027 budget built purely on 2026 actuals misses the things that officially change at the new year. Update these while you draft:

**Social Security.** Benefits rise 3.6% in January 2027 — the cost-of-living adjustment the Social Security Administration announced in October. If you or a partner collect benefits, adjust that income line up.

**Retirement contributions.** For 2026, the 401(k) employee limit is $24,500, with an $8,000 catch-up if you're 50 or older — and a higher $11,250 catch-up if you're 60 to 63. The IRA limit is $7,500, plus a $1,100 catch-up. The IRS typically announces 2027 limits in mid-November, so check before you lock your contribution line; if you budget to max out, the number will likely move.

**HSA contributions.** The 2027 limits are already out: $4,500 for self-only coverage and $9,000 for family coverage, plus the unchanged $1,000 catch-up at 55.

**Premiums and subscriptions.** Health insurance, auto, and home premiums rarely carry over flat. Use renewal notices where you have them. And pull a year of subscription charges while you're in the data — the services you forgot you pay for are the easiest line items you will ever cut.

## Step 5: Turn irregular expenses into a monthly number

Take your annual irregular total — say it's $6,000 — and treat it as $500 a month, every month, moved into a holding category or a separate savings account. When the car registration or the December gift season arrives, the money is already there.

This one habit removes most of the panic from budgeting. The expenses were never actually surprises. They were just unscheduled.

## Step 6: Decide the savings line on purpose

Whatever is left after spending is your savings capacity — but don't leave it as a remainder. Make it a line item: retirement contributions, taxable investing, cash buffer. A budget where saving is a deliberate number, not leftovers, is the budget that actually builds wealth.

## Step 7: Schedule the review, not just the budget

An annual budget is not a January ritual. It is a standing agreement you check against reality. Pick a cadence — monthly works for most people — and review budget versus actuals: which categories ran hot, which ran cold, whether the irregular fund is keeping pace.

Two rules for reviews. First, adjust the budget when life changes, not when a single month goes sideways. Second, never treat a blown category as a moral failing. The budget is an instrument panel, not a report card.

## The November advantage

Do this now, in November, and three things happen. You catch the subscription renewals and premium increases before they hit. You walk into the holiday season with gifts already funded instead of charged. And on January 1, while everyone else is guessing at resolutions, your 2027 budget is already running — built on twelve months of evidence about how you actually live.
